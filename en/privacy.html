Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 8, 2026

Brothrone Travel ("this blog") values the privacy of its visitors. This policy explains what information is collected and how it is used.

1. Information We Collect

When you post a comment, this blog stores your chosen nickname, comment, posting time, and a hash of a deletion token. Nicknames and comments are public. No account or email address is required. The following data may also be automatically collected through third-party services:

  • Visit statistics (page views, session duration, referral sources, etc.)
  • Device information (browser type, operating system, screen resolution, etc.)
  • Approximate location (country and city level only)

This information is collected via Google Analytics and Google AdSense solely for the purpose of improving the blog and delivering ads.

2. Cookies

This blog uses cookies provided by Google Analytics and Google AdSense. These cookies help improve user experience and enable personalized advertising.

You can disable cookies through your browser settings; however, some functionality may be limited as a result.

3. Third-Party Services

This blog uses the following third-party services. You can review how each service handles data via their respective privacy policies.

  • Google Analytics — Traffic analysis and user behavior statistics.
    Google Privacy Policy →
  • Google AdSense — Ad delivery.
    Google Privacy Policy →

4. Advertising

This blog displays ads through Google AdSense. Google may use cookies to serve personalized ads based on your browsing history and interests.

You can opt out of personalized ads via Google Ad Settings.

Comments

Comments are stored using Cloudflare D1 and checked for automated spam using Cloudflare Turnstile. We do not store raw IP addresses in the comments database. A temporary, secret-keyed identifier limits repeated submissions; it expires after 24 hours and is removed on a subsequent successful submission. Cloudflare processes connection information to provide hosting and spam protection. See the Cloudflare Privacy Policy.

Your browser stores a random deletion key so you can remove your own comments without logging in. Clearing browser storage removes that key. Comments remain until deleted by you or the blog administrator. To request removal if you no longer have the key, use the contact page. Please do not include personal or sensitive information in public comments.

5. Contact

If you have any questions about this privacy policy, please reach out via the contact page.
