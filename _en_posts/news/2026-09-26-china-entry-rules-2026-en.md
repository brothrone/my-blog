China's entry rules **changed on September 15.**
A State Council regulation on exit and entry, published in July, is now in force, and it states that border officers can ask for **"electronic data" as well as documents.**

Many travellers now visit China visa-free.
**Visa-free does not mean no immigration check**, so this applies to them too.

## What matters for travellers

I read the [regulation text on the Chinese government site](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202607/content_7077173.htm) (State Council Decree No. 841, in Chinese).
Most of its 19 articles cover Chinese citizens leaving the country and exit-entry agencies. Two articles matter for foreign visitors.

| Article | What it says |
| --- | --- |
| Art. 3 | When verifying identity and purpose, officers may ask questions and require you to show or provide **documents, materials, electronic data** and other information. Travellers must cooperate |
| Art. 3 | False materials or statements can lead to refused entry |
| Art. 5 | Foreigners who give false materials or statements when applying for a visa or at the border can be **barred for 1 to 5 years** |
| Art. 19 | In force from September 15, 2026 |

## What is "electronic data"?

The regulation does not define it.

As reported by Singapore's CNA (and cited by Korea's [Travel Times](https://www.traveltimes.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=500439)), a Chinese immigration lawyer said it **could include information stored on a phone**, but that checks would be **selective, as needed, rather than routine** for everyone.
He also noted that much of what is written down was already happening in practice, so most tourists are unlikely to notice a big change.

So the fair summary is: **not "every phone gets searched", but there is now an explicit legal basis to ask, and you are expected to cooperate.**

## Visa-free entry for South Koreans

From the [Chinese Embassy in Korea](https://kr.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw/hzlxzyw/zgqz/202511/t20251104_11746485.htm) (in Chinese):

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Who | Ordinary South Korean passport holders (one of several countries covered) |
| Stay | Up to 30 days |
| Purpose | Tourism, business, visiting family or friends, exchanges, transit |
| Until | **December 31, 2026** |

The policy currently runs **to the end of this year.** If you are planning China for January or later, watch for an extension.

## Before you go

China's foreign ministry also advises visa-free visitors to carry proof of the purpose of their visit.

| Bring | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Return or onward flight booking | Shows you leave within 30 days |
| Hotel booking | Confirms where you stay and why |
| For business: invitation letter or company documents | Confirms the purpose |
| Offline screenshots | Your data may not work right after landing |

Most importantly:
**answer questions at immigration truthfully.**
The new regulation explicitly allows a 1–5 year ban for false statements.
Hiding the real purpose of a trip, or showing made-up bookings, now carries a much bigger risk.

For ordinary tourists this is not a reason to worry.
It just makes sense to travel ready to show things if asked.
