If the taxes on your latest Japan flight looked a little higher than before, there is a reason.
**Japan's departure tax (the International Tourist Tax) tripled from ¥1,000 to ¥3,000 on July 1.**

Practically every Japan ticket you buy now includes the ¥3,000.
But **some tickets bought before June can end up paying ¥3,000 too**, so here is how that works.

## How much, and from when

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Name | International Tourist Tax (国際観光旅客税) |
| Amount | **¥1,000 → ¥3,000** per departure |
| Effective | **Departures from July 1, 2026** |
| Who pays | Anyone leaving Japan by air or sea, whatever their nationality or purpose |
| How | Included in the ticket price and collected by the airline |

You do not pay anything at the airport. **It sits in the taxes and fees on your ticket**, which makes it easy to miss.

It is ¥2,000 more per person, so a family of four pays **¥8,000 more** per trip, or ¥12,000 in total.
It applies every time you leave, so frequent visitors will notice it.

## What about tickets bought before June?

As a rule, **tickets contracted (issued) by June 30 keep the old ¥1,000 rate even for departures after July 1.**
But the National Tax Agency's Q&A lists **exceptions** where ¥3,000 applies:

| Situation | Rate |
| --- | --- |
| Issued by June 30, departing after July 1 on the original date | ¥1,000 |
| Issued by June 30, but **departure date changed on or after July 1** | **¥3,000** |
| Open ticket with no departure date, date fixed on or after July 1 | **¥3,000** |
| Ticket whose terms say the tax is collected separately from the fare | **¥3,000** |

So if you bought a cheap autumn or winter Japan ticket before the summer, **changing the date now may add a tax top-up**,
on top of any change fee.

## Who does not pay

| Exempt | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| **Children under 2** | |
| **Transit passengers leaving within 24 hours** | Passing through Japan to another country |
| Aircraft and ship crew | |
| Passengers returned to Japan without landing abroad, e.g. due to weather | |

On a ticket that connects through Japan (say Seoul–Tokyo–Hawaii), **you do not pay if you leave within 24 hours**.
Stay longer than that and the tax applies.

## How to check

Open the taxes and fees breakdown on the payment screen or your e-ticket receipt and the Japan departure tax appears as a separate line.
The label varies by airline.

If you want to change the date on an older ticket priced at ¥1,000, **ask the airline or agent whether extra tax will be charged before you change it.**

## Where the money goes

According to the Japan Tourism Agency leaflet, the extra revenue goes to **faster border procedures** (automated bag drops, walk-through gates and the like), measures against overcrowding at tourist spots, and developing regional attractions.
