**ETIAS, Europe's electronic travel authorisation, is now unlikely to start this year.** Around July, the EU removed the "last quarter of 2026" launch window from the official ETIAS website, which now says only that the start date will be announced several months in advance. The Financial Times reported that eu-LISA, the EU agency running the system, acknowledged a launch by the end of the year was no longer feasible.

Europe's border process has still changed, though. What affects visitors today is the **Entry/Exit System (EES), fully live since April**, and if your trip includes Britain, the **UK ETA**. Here are all three in one place.

## At a glance

| System | Where | Status | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ETIAS | 30 European countries | **Not in operation**, no start date | €20 (planned) |
| EES | 29 Schengen countries | **Fully operational since April 10, 2026** | Free |
| UK ETA | United Kingdom | In force for many nationalities, including South Koreans since January 8, 2025 | £20 |

## ETIAS: nothing to apply for yet

ETIAS is a pre-travel online authorisation for visa-exempt visitors, similar to the US ESTA. The planned rules:

| Item | Planned details |
| --- | --- |
| Fee | €20 (free for under-18s and over-70s, who still need to apply) |
| Validity | Three years or until the passport expires, whichever is sooner |
| Countries | 30 European countries, including the Schengen area |
| Where to apply | Official EU site, travel-europe.europa.eu/etias |

**The official site is not taking applications.** Search results are full of sites offering "ETIAS applications"; since the system does not exist yet, do not pay anyone. The EU says it will announce the date months ahead, and the industry expects 2027, but nothing has been confirmed.

The delay is tied to EES. ETIAS relies on the EES database, and reports say the long queues at several airports after EES went fully live made it hard to stabilise both systems at once.

## EES: applies the moment you arrive

EES replaces passport stamps with an electronic record of entries and exits. As the [European Commission announced](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/news/entryexit-system-will-become-fully-operational-10-april-2026-2026-03-30_en), it has been in full use at every Schengen external border since April 10, 2026, and passports are no longer stamped.

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| First entry | Passport scan, facial image and four fingerprints |
| Children under 12 | Facial image only, no fingerprints |
| How long it is kept | Three years after your last trip; return within that time and you only verify face or fingerprints |
| Length of stay | The 90/180-day rule is calculated automatically |

Registration adds time at the border on your first trip, so **expect longer immigration queues**. Since full launch there have been reports of multi-hour waits at some airports, including in Greece. If you are connecting to a train or domestic flight right after landing, leave a generous buffer.

Because the system counts your days automatically, long multi-country trips need a proper check that you stay within 90 days in any 180-day period.

## UK ETA: required if London is on the route

Britain is outside Schengen and runs its own Electronic Travel Authorisation. According to the [Home Office factsheet](https://homeofficemedia.blog.gov.uk/electronic-travel-authorisation-eta-factsheet-april-2026/), **the fee rose from £16 to £20 on April 8, 2026**.

An ETA allows multiple visits of up to six months each over two years, or until your passport expires. The rules for **airside transit without passing UK border control** have changed before and depend on the airport, so check your own itinerary on gov.uk before you fly.

## Checklist

| Trip | What to do now |
| --- | --- |
| Schengen only (Paris, Rome, Switzerland…) | No ETIAS. Allow time for EES registration on first entry |
| Includes London | Get a UK ETA before travel (£20) |
| London to mainland Europe | ETA plus EES registration at your first Schengen check (for Eurostar, that is at the French border control at London St Pancras) |
| Europe next year or later | Watch for the official ETIAS start date |

If I repeated my January 2024 route of London, Paris, Switzerland and Italy today, I would need a UK ETA before leaving and would register in EES at the first Schengen check, which on Eurostar happens in London rather than Paris. No ETIAS yet.
