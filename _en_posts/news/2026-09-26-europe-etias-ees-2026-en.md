**ETIAS, Europe's electronic travel authorisation, is now unlikely to start this year.**

Around July, the EU quietly removed "last quarter of 2026" from the official ETIAS website.
All it says now is that the start date will be announced a few months in advance.
The Financial Times also reported that eu-LISA, the EU agency running the system, admitted a launch this year was no longer realistic.

That does not mean nothing has changed.
What applies right now is the **Entry/Exit System (EES), fully live since April**, and if London is on your route, the **UK ETA**.

The names are confusingly similar, so here are all three in one place.

## At a glance

| System | Where | Status | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ETIAS | 30 European countries | **Not started**, no date | €20 (planned) |
| EES | 29 Schengen countries | **Fully live since April 10, 2026** | Free |
| UK ETA | United Kingdom | Required for many nationalities, including South Koreans since January 8, 2025 | £20 |

## ETIAS: nothing to apply for yet

ETIAS is a pre-travel online authorisation, like the US ESTA, for visitors who do not need a visa.

The plan so far:

| Item | Planned |
| --- | --- |
| Fee | €20 (free under 18 and over 70, but you still apply) |
| Validity | Three years or until your passport expires |
| Countries | 30, including the Schengen area |
| Where | Official EU site, travel-europe.europa.eu/etias |

**The official site is not taking applications.**
Search for it and you will find plenty of "ETIAS application" services.
The system does not exist yet, so there is nothing to pay for.

The EU says it will announce the date months ahead. The industry expects 2027, but nothing is confirmed.

The delay is tied to EES.
ETIAS runs on the EES database, and after EES went fully live, long queues at several airports made it hard to stabilise both systems at once, according to multiple reports.

## EES: applies the moment you arrive

EES **replaces passport stamps with an electronic record** of when you enter and leave.
As the [European Commission announced](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/news/entryexit-system-will-become-fully-operational-10-april-2026-2026-03-30_en), it has been in full use at every Schengen external border since April 10, 2026. No more stamps.

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| First entry | Passport scan + face photo + four fingerprints |
| Under 12 | Face photo only |
| Record kept | Three years after your last visit; return within that and it is just a face or fingerprint check |
| Days in Schengen | The 90/180 rule is counted automatically |

That extra step means **longer immigration queues on your first trip.**
Since full launch there have been reports of multi-hour waits at some airports, including in Greece.
If you are connecting to a train or domestic flight after landing, leave a generous buffer.

And because your days are now counted automatically, check that long multi-country trips stay within **90 days in any 180**.

## UK ETA: required for London

The UK is not in Schengen, so it runs its own Electronic Travel Authorisation separate from EES and ETIAS.
According to the [Home Office factsheet](https://homeofficemedia.blog.gov.uk/electronic-travel-authorisation-eta-factsheet-april-2026/), **the fee went up from £16 to £20 on April 8, 2026.**

One ETA covers multiple trips over two years (or until your passport expires), up to six months each time.

The rules for **airside transit without going through UK border control** have changed before and depend on the airport.
If you are only connecting, check your itinerary on gov.uk before you fly.

## What does your trip need?

| Trip | What to do now |
| --- | --- |
| Schengen only (Paris, Rome, Switzerland…) | No ETIAS. Allow time for EES on first entry |
| Includes London | UK ETA before you travel (£20) |
| London → mainland Europe | ETA + EES at your first Schengen check (on Eurostar, that is in London) |
| Europe next year or later | Watch for the ETIAS start date |

If I did my January 2024 route again (London, Paris, Switzerland, Italy), it would look like this:
a UK ETA before leaving, then EES registration at the first Schengen border check.
On Eurostar, that check happens at the **French border control at London St Pancras**, not in Paris.
No ETIAS yet.
