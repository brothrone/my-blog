This one matters if you stay **with friends, in a short-term rental or in a guesthouse in China rather than a hotel**.
In those cases you have always had to **register at the local police (public security) office within 24 hours of arriving**.

**Since September 21, you can do it online anywhere in China.**
China's National Immigration Administration (NIA) announced it in Notice No. 7 of 2026.

## Hotels handle it for you

First, to be clear: **if you stay in a hotel, you do not need to do anything.**
The hotel takes your passport details at check-in and registers you.

It applies in these cases:

| Where you stay | Registration |
| --- | --- |
| Hotels and licensed guesthouses | **Done by the property** (nothing to do) |
| A friend's or relative's home | **You or your host** |
| Unlicensed short-term rentals, Airbnb-style stays | **You or your host** |
| A home you own | You |

The deadline is **within 24 hours of moving in**.
It comes from Article 39 of China's Exit and Entry Administration Law and applies to visa-free visitors too.

## Where to do it

| Channel | |
| --- | --- |
| NIA government services website | |
| "NIA 12367" (移民局12367) app | |
| "NIA 12367" WeChat mini program | |
| Alipay mini program | |
| In person at the local police office | Still available |

Go to "Services for foreigners" (外国人服务) → "Accommodation registration" (住宿登记), verify your identity, and follow the steps.
Afterwards, check "Registration records" (登记记录查询) to confirm it went through.

**Online registration has the same legal effect as registering in person.**

## Your host has to help the first time

One catch:
**the first registration at an address has to be done with the host's help.**
The NIA says this is because hosts know the property details better.

Travellers can only do it alone in two cases:
- You have **registered online at the same address before**
- You are staying **in a home you own**

So if you are staying with a friend or in a rental, **ask your host before you arrive to do the online registration with you.**
The host can be a Chinese citizen or a foreigner.

## What if you don't?

Under Article 76 of the same law, **the foreigner or the host can be given a warning and may be fined.**
If you get stuck, call the 12367 hotline or ask at the service window of a local police station.

## Also worth knowing

The service was piloted from March 20 in seven regions (Hebei, Liaoning, Zhejiang, Hubei, Guangxi, Chongqing and Sichuan) before going nationwide.

**China's entry checks also changed on September 15.** Officers can ask for electronic data when checking the purpose of your visit, so keep your accommodation bookings handy.
