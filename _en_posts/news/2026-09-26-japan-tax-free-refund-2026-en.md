Tax-free shopping in Japan works differently from November 1. Today you show your passport at the till and pay the price without consumption tax. Under the new system you **pay the full tax-inclusive price, then get the tax back after customs confirms at departure that the goods are leaving Japan**.

According to [Japan's National Tax Agency](https://www.nta.go.jp/publication/pamph/shohi/menzei/201805/format/002.htm), the change was set in the fiscal 2025 tax reform and takes effect on November 1, 2026. There is no transition period in which both systems run side by side. For anyone visiting Japan this winter, the biggest change is not in the shops but at the airport on the day you fly home.

## What changes

| Item | Until October 31 | From November 1 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paying in store | Price without consumption tax | Full tax-inclusive price |
| When you get the tax back | Not needed (exempt at the till) | After customs confirmation at departure |
| General goods vs consumables | Separate categories | Distinction removed |
| ¥500,000 cap on consumables | Applies | Removed |
| Sealed packaging for consumables | Required | Not required |
| Minimum spend | ¥5,000 before tax, same store, same day | Unchanged |
| Deadline to take goods out | — | Within 90 days of purchase |

The refund is the consumption tax you paid: 10% on most goods and 8% on items at the reduced rate, such as food. On a ¥30,000 purchase before tax, that means paying ¥3,000 extra up front and getting it back later.

Dropping the consumables limit and the sealed bags makes shopping simpler. But because **you have to be able to show the goods when you leave**, anything you have eaten or used up in Japan is unlikely to be refunded.

For items costing ¥1 million or more before tax, the purchase record includes details that identify the specific item, such as a serial number. Keep the receipt and product details together if you are buying a watch or bag at that level.

## On departure day

The [Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO)](https://www.japan.travel/en/us/blog/everything-you-need-to-know-japans-new-tax-free-shopping-system/) describes the airport process as follows.

| Step | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Scan your passport at a tax-free kiosk in the international departure lobby |
| 2 | Check the result on screen |
| 3a | **Green**: confirmation complete. Check in your bags with the airline |
| 3b | **Red**: take all your tax-free items to the customs counter for inspection |
| 4 | Once export is confirmed, the store or its refund agent processes the refund |

The step that is easiest to miss: **go to the kiosk before you check your bags**. If a suitcase containing tax-free purchases goes into the hold first, you cannot complete the confirmation and you will not get the tax back.

If the screen shows red, a customs officer checks the goods in person. Confirmation is done per receipt, meaning per purchase record. Guidance and press reports say that **if even one tax-free item on a receipt is missing, the other items on that receipt may also be excluded from the refund**. Worth remembering if you have already handed out some of the gifts.

## How and when you are refunded

You do not get cash at the airport. The refund comes from **the store where you bought the goods or a refund agent working for it**. Whether it goes back to your card or another method, and how long it takes, varies by store and agent. The simplest way to be sure is to ask at the till how the refund will be paid and what details they need.

Some stores or agents may deduct a fee, so on a large purchase check the expected refund amount shown on the receipt. That depends on how each store runs the process rather than on the rules themselves.

## Before you travel

| Prepare | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Keep tax-free items in your carry-on or somewhere easy to reach | If the kiosk shows red, customs needs to see all of them |
| Keep receipts and purchase records | Checks are done receipt by receipt |
| Arrive at the airport with extra time | The kiosk and any inspection come before check-in |
| Note which card you paid with | Some stores refund to the payment method |
| Hand out gifts after you leave Japan | One missing item can exclude the whole receipt |

This checklist is based on how the system works; it is not part of the official rules. Kiosk locations and procedures may differ by airport, so follow the signs at your departure airport on the day.

This is separate from JESTA, Japan's planned electronic travel authorisation, which is expected from 2028 at the earliest. JESTA has nothing to do with the tax-free change and is not needed for trips this year or next.
